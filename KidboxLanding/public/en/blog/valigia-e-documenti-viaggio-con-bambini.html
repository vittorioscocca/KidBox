Preparing a trip with children has one feature: the forgotten thing is always the one you can't buy when you get there. The child's ID, the prescription medicine, the soft toy nobody sleeps without. And usually it's all in one parent's head, packing for four people the night before departure.

A written, shared checklist solves both problems.

Three weeks before: documents

This is the part you can't fix at the last minute.

  • ID documents for everyone: valid, and not expiring during the trip. For abroad, check whether an ID card is enough or a passport is required, and whether the country requires remaining validity. Children's documents expire more often: more in children's ID cards and passports.
  • Children travelling without one parent: many countries require a consent letter or official authorisation for a minor travelling abroad without both parents. Check current requirements with your own authorities and the destination country well in advance.
  • Health cover: within the EU the European Health Insurance Card applies; for other countries consider travel insurance.
  • Visas and entry requirements, where applicable.

In KidBox's wallet the children's ID cards, passports and health cards are saved encrypted, with expiry dates highlighted, and PDF tickets are read by the AI and shown cleanly, available on both parents' phones. Digital copies help if something is lost, but don't replace the originals.

One week before: the shared list

A shared "Trip" to-do list, with every item assigned to a parent:

  • the children's regular medicines, with prescriptions, plus a basic kit (fever reducer, plasters, antiseptic) as advised by your paediatrician
  • chargers, plug adapters, power banks
  • entertainment for the journey: books, games, headphones, content downloaded for offline use
  • car seats, your own or for the hire car, if needed
  • accommodation and car hire confirmations saved

Whoever takes an item ticks it. Nobody assumes the other did it.

The night before: the bags

  • One bag per child, packed with the children from six or seven up, from a list that's the same for everyone
  • Children's hand luggage with a full change of clothes, a snack, the favourite toy
  • Documents and tickets in one place, decided in advance: one pocket, one envelope

A fixed packing list for the children, saved once, gets reused every trip: just update it for the season.

Departure day

The final check, at the door: documents, tickets, phones, medicines, house keys. Five items, checked aloud by both parents.

After the trip

A note of what was missing and what was useless makes the next trip's list better. After two or three trips, the family checklist is close to perfect.

In short

Three weeks before: documents, checking requirements and expiry dates; one week before: the shared list with items assigned; the night before: a bag per child and documents in one place; at the door: five items. You'll still leave in a rush. But you won't have to turn back.
